My Trip to Japan

Trip Story: My trip to Japan began the moment I stepped off the plane at Narita — the air felt different, crisp and orderly, as if the country itself had a sense of discipline. I spent the first few days in Tokyo, a city that somehow manages to be overwhelming and perfectly organized at the same time. I started in Shinjuku, weaving through neon-lit alleys and ducking into a tiny ramen shop where a bowl of tonkotsu broth warmed me from the inside out. The next morning I visited Senso-ji Temple in Asakusa — even at 7 a.m., vendors were already setting up their stalls, and the smell of incense drifted across the courtyard. In the afternoons I lost myself in Akihabara, surrounded by floors of electronics and anime merchandise, and in the evenings I wandered Shibuya, watching the famous crossing flood with people from every direction all at once. Tokyo never really slept, and neither did I. On the fourth day, I took the Shinkansen toward Kawaguchiko to see Mount Fuji. Nothing quite prepares you for the moment the mountain appears through the train window — this perfectly symmetrical cone rising above the clouds, enormous and ancient and calm. At Lake Kawaguchi, I rented a bicycle and rode along the shore as the early morning mist lifted off the water. Fuji's reflection shimmered on the surface like a painting. I hiked the Aokigahara trail for a few hours, moving through dense forest where the only sounds were birdsong and my own footsteps on volcanic rock. On my last evening, I sat at a lakeside café, drinking green tea and watching the sun drop behind Fuji's ridge. The sky turned amber, then deep purple. I thought about how the Japanese word mono no aware — the bittersweet beauty of things passing — described that moment perfectly. Japan gave me two sides of itself: the electric, restless energy of Tokyo, and the silent, timeless majesty of Fuji. I left with a full camera roll and an even fuller sense of wonder.
